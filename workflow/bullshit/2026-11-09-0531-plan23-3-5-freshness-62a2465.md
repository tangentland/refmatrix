### SKETCHY s1: 23.3 names three daemon `_sync_paths` sites; HEAD has five, two of them hook-driven {#s1}

**File:** [[task-23.3-sync-between-file-yield#r3]], [[task-23.3-sync-between-file-yield#r7]],
[[task-23.3-sync-between-file-yield#r8]], [[task-23.3-sync-between-file-yield#ac-9]].

**What:** `_op_flush_queue` (`daemon.py:3453`, which the Stop hook reaches through
`rmx sync --flush-queue`) and `_op_flush_queue_async`'s runner thread (`:3466-3497`, which every
PostToolUse edit reaches through `--async`) both call `flush_queue → _sync_paths` under an outer
`with d._store_lock:`. Both have existed since 2026-05-16 (`25735176`, `8f6b1755`). The spec
omits them, and it omits the non-daemon caller `cli.py:8743`.

**Why it's sketchy:** Once `bind` is REQUIRED (R3), an implementer has two options and the spec
picks neither:
- Pass `daemon_bind(d)` inside the kept outer hold. The `_FairLock` is not re-entrant, so this
  deadlocks (23.2's `LockDeadlock` in tests, a hang live).
- Pass something outside `_sync_mutex`. A flush then interleaves with a watch flush step by step,
  which is exactly ns23-r3-3's duplicate-evidence hazard.

The async runner is also a thread with no `_inflight_ops` entry. 23.5 R4 then reports it as
"untracked thread rmxd-async-flush".

**Fix:**
- R3/R7/R8 list all five sites plus `cli.py:8743`. Every daemon site drops its outer hold, takes
  `_sync_mutex`, passes `daemon_bind(d)` and logs a start line.
- The async runner enters `Daemon._inflight("sync")` (23.5 R4's name list already has `sync`).
- AC-9 adds a pair of a `flush_queue_async` runner and a watch flush.
- AC-7 names the flush-queue tests.

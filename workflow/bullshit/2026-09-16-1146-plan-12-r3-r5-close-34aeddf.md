## The three questions rounds 4 and 5 asked, answered {#questions}

- **Can the hash cache return a wrong answer?** Yes, and it did — reproduced. Not by mtime
  collision (2000 alternating same-size rewrites on APFS: **0** identical `mtime_ns`) but by an
  mtime-preserving restore at the same size, which returned a cache HIT carrying the old hash. The
  failure direction is a MISS: `behind_code` reads False and the gate silently does not fire,
  which is bug-039's blind spot rebuilt inside bug-039's own detector — and `(mtime, size)` as a
  content identity is bug-037 exactly, the reason `tools/pyc_guard.py` exists. Fixed with
  `ctime_ns` + `ino`; both restore shapes now miss. Cache growth measured at ~145 B/entry, about
  one entry per deploy — correctly left unbounded.
- **Is the partition walk too expensive for the hub tick?** No. Measured 4.68 ms at 3 partitions,
  15.95 at 10, 49.31 at 30 — ~1.6 ms/partition. Fleet maximum is 3 (`cliquedb`: `global`,
  `cliquedb`, `sessions-cliquedb`). ~40 ms across 8 stores against a 15 s `WATCHDOG_INTERVAL_S` is
  a 0.27% duty cycle. Revisit near 30 partitions.
- **Does the NULL-`code_hash`-is-behind rule flood on the day it lands?** No, and this is the one I
  could settle absolutely: read-only DuckDB against every root's lock-free snapshot shows
  `derive_stamps` **does not exist in any of the eight stores**. No rows, so no NULL rows. Deploy
  day is `never_stamped` → `derive_unstamped` → cold, as designed. The rule can only bite a store
  stamped between `20a4ba3` and `43817a9`, which needs an intermediate sha deployed; the deploy
  tree is still 0.71.0.
